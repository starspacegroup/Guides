## Rule

A landing page states its audience and its outcome in the headline, backs them with specifics a reader can check, and offers one main action. A claim with no proof behind it is left out, with a note to add it when the proof exists.

## Why

Visitors decide in a few seconds whether a page is for them. "The future of collaboration" could be anything. "Shared budgets for couples, set up in ten minutes" tells the right reader to stay and the wrong reader to leave, and both outcomes are good.

Invented proof is the other failure. A made-up user count, an "as seen in" row with no coverage behind it, or a testimonial nobody gave works until one person checks. After that, nothing on the page is believed.

## Must

- Name the audience and the outcome in the headline.
- Put checkable specifics in the line below it: a number, a time, a named integration, a price.
- Offer one main call to action, and make it the next step of what the headline promised.
- Match the page to what brought the visitor. If the ad or the link said "free template", the page leads with the free template.
- Replace each unproven claim with `TODO(owner): add when we have it`. Ship the page without it.
- Write for the reader's problem before you describe your features.

## Before and after

Before:

> Next-generation platform for modern teams. Unlock productivity with our powerful, flexible, AI-driven solution. Get started, book a demo, read the docs, or join the community.

After:

> Release notes that write themselves, for teams that ship every week. Connect your repository, and get a draft from your merged pull requests in under a minute. Free for public repositories.
>
> **Draft my release notes**

The second version names the reader, states the outcome, gives three facts that can be checked, and has one action.

## Should

- Read the page aloud. A sentence you would not say to a person across a table should go.
- Use the customer's own words where you have them.
- Put the price, or the fact that it is free, where people look for it.
- Show the product. A real screenshot is better than an abstract illustration.
- Answer the first objection directly, near the top.
- Keep secondary actions quiet, as plain links.
- End with the same call to action as the top.

## Gotchas

- Three equal buttons are no call to action. People choose none.
- Words such as "powerful", "seamless", and "robust" carry no information. Replace each with the fact it stands for, or delete it.
- A headline tuned for the company ("We are the leading...") describes the wrong party. The reader is the subject.
- A page that promises what the product will do next quarter creates support requests today.
- Statistics age. Date them, and review them.

## Validation checklist

- A stranger can say who the page is for, and what they would get, after five seconds.
- Every number and named claim on the page has a source you can show.
- There is exactly one primary button style, used for one action.
- The first screen at phone width shows the headline, the supporting line, and the action.
- No `TODO(owner)` item was filled with a guess.
